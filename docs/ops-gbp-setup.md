# Google Business Profile — Setup & Operating Checklist

**Owner:** Luke Petzer. **Written:** 25 July 2026. **Status:** not yet claimed.

This is a do-it-yourself checklist. Everything here is done by Luke in the Google Business
Profile dashboard (google.com/business, or by searching "my business" while signed in to the
Google account that should own the profile). Nothing in this doc is automated by the site.

One rule before you start: **use the Google account you will still control in five years.**
Transferring ownership later is possible but tedious. Use the lpwebstudio.co.za Workspace
account if there is one, not a personal Gmail.

Google renames categories and moves buttons every few months. If a label below does not match
what you see, pick the nearest match rather than the closest-sounding wrong one.

---

## 1. Category — the single most important field

**Primary category: `Software company`.**

The primary category is the biggest ranking and classification signal on the profile. It tells
Google which searches you are eligible for, and it shapes what kind of lead walks in.

| Category | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Software company** | **Use this.** Matches the positioning: custom systems, ordering portals, client platforms. Attracts people looking for something built, not something designed. |
| Website designer | **Do not use.** It is the higher-volume category, which is exactly the problem — it is the category people search when they want a R5,000 brochure site. It would retrain the profile toward the business we just spent a rework getting out of, and those leads waste calls. |
| Computer consultant | Reasonable **secondary**. Catches "custom software Cape Town" style intent. |
| E-commerce service | Reasonable **secondary** — the ordering platform genuinely is one. |
| Internet marketing service | Skip. Wrong business entirely, attracts SEO/ads enquiries. |

Add **at most two or three** secondary categories. Every extra category dilutes the primary
signal. Secondary categories can be changed later at no cost, so start narrow.

## 3. Verification

Google now usually asks for **video verification** in South Africa rather than a postcard.

Record one continuous, unedited video (no cuts, no stopping) showing:

1. The outside of the building / street where you work, ideally with something locating it.
2. Walking to your workspace — desk, monitors, the actual setup.
3. Proof you run the business: the LP Web Studio admin dashboard open on screen, an invoice with
   the business name on it, or business banking on screen.
4. Your face at the end if prompted.

Two minutes maximum, film in landscape, do not narrate over silence — Google reviewers are
checking continuity, not production value. If it is rejected, you can resubmit; the usual cause
is a cut in the footage or nothing tying the workspace to the business name.

## 4. Photos

Photos are the difference between a profile people trust and a profile people scroll past.
Aim for **12–15 to start**, then add one or two a month.

- [ ] **Logo** — square, 720×720, PNG on a solid background. `public/my-logo.svg` exported to PNG.
- [ ] **Cover** — 1024×575. The dark hero aesthetic from the site works here.
- [ ] **Founder photo** — `public/luke-petzer.jpg` already exists and is good. Solo business:
      people want to see who they are dealing with before they call.
- [ ] **Product screenshots (5–6)** — the ordering portal catalogue, a cart/checkout screen, the
      admin dashboard with orders in it, the portal open on a phone, the lp-os dashboard. Screens
      on real devices (laptop on a desk, phone in hand) outperform flat screenshots.
- [ ] **Work-in-progress (2–3)** — desk setup, code on screen, a whiteboard with a workflow
      sketched out. It reads as real work rather than a stock library.
- [ ] **One short video (under 30 seconds)** — a client placing an order in the portal, start to
      finish. This is the single most persuasive asset available and almost nobody in the
      category uploads one.

Practical notes: name the files descriptively before upload (`lp-web-studio-ordering-portal-
admin-dashboard.jpg`) — Google strips EXIF and geotags, so file naming is the only text signal
left. No stock photography. No screenshots containing a real client's prices, customer names or
order values without their written OK.

## 5. Reviews

A profile with zero reviews converts worse than no profile at all. Getting the first three is
the whole job; after that it compounds.

**Get the review link first:** in the dashboard, "Ask for reviews" → copy the short link
(`g.page/r/...`). Save it somewhere you can paste it from your phone in three seconds. Asking
someone to "search for us on Google and leave a review" loses most of them.

### Order to ask, in sequence

1. **Gio (Cafe Crave) — first, this week.** He already gave the written testimonial on the site,
   so he has said the words once and will say them again. He is the warmest ask you have.
2. Any client whose system is currently live and working — ask at a moment of delivered value
   (the week after launch, or right after you fix something fast for them), never cold.
3. Pilot partners on lp-os, once they have used it for a month.

### WhatsApp message to Gio

> Hey Gio — quick favour. I've just set up the Google Business Profile for LP Web Studio and it's
> sitting there with no reviews, which doesn't help when new clients look me up. You already said
> something kind about the work a while back — would you mind putting a version of it here?
> [link] Takes about a minute, and it genuinely makes a difference on my side. No stress if you'd
> rather not.

Do not send a pre-written review for them to paste. Google can detect near-duplicate text, and
it reads as manufactured to anyone who checks. If Gio asks what to mention, say: what the problem
was before, and what changed after.

### Rules

- Never offer a discount, credit or anything else for a review. It violates Google's policy and
  is grounds for review removal or profile suspension.
- Never filter — asking only happy clients to review is fine; sending unhappy ones to a private
  form first is "review gating" and is against policy.
- **Reply to every review within 48 hours**, including the good ones. Replies are public and
  visible to prospects, and they are a ranking factor.
- Reply to a negative review once, briefly, factually, and take it offline: "Sorry this was your
  experience — I'd like to sort it out. I'll call you today." Never argue in public.

**Target:** 3 reviews in the first month, 5 by the end of the outreach period, then 1–2 a month.

## 6. Posting cadence — 4 weeks

One post a week, Tuesday or Wednesday morning. "What's new" posts stay visible for six months,
so this builds up rather than evaporating. Each post: 150–300 words of body, one photo, one CTA
button. Keep the site's voice — plain English, no hype, no emoji.

Post every week even when nobody engages. The profile being *active* is a signal in itself, and
the posts are what a prospect reads after your cold email lands.

**Photo:** ordering portal open on a laptop
**CTA:** Learn more → `https://lpwebstudio.co.za/products`

> Most wholesalers we speak to take orders three ways at once: WhatsApp, email, and a rep phoning
> them in. Someone then re-types all of it into a spreadsheet, and that person becomes the single
> point of failure for the whole operation.

> We build the thing that replaces it — a branded ordering portal your customers log into. They
> see their own pricing, their own catalogue, and their order lands in your admin dashboard
> without anyone re-typing it.

> Live and taking real orders now. There's a demo on the products page.

### Week 2 — The cost of the manual version

**CTA:** Learn more → `https://lpwebstudio.co.za/products`

> A quick exercise. Count how many hours a week someone in your business spends moving order

> accounting, accounting into a delivery note.

Rotate the same four angles with new detail: what we build → what manual costs → a common
objection answered → a direct offer. Add a fifth type once there is a client win to write up
(with permission, and no client figures published without Luke's explicit sign-off).

## 7. Ongoing checklist

**Weekly:** post once. Reply to any new review within 48 hours. Answer any Q&A that appears.

**Monthly:** add 1–2 photos. Check the Performance tab for which search terms are bringing calls —
if it is filling up with "web design" style queries, the category mix needs tightening.

**Quarterly:** re-read the description against current positioning. Check nobody has suggested an
edit to the profile (Google lets the public suggest changes, and they sometimes go live silently
— the dashboard flags them under "Updates from Google" or in the profile's edit history).

**Seed the Q&A yourself.** You can post a question on your own profile and answer it from the
business account. Do three:

1. "What does a custom system cost?" → "Custom builds start at R35,000. The B2B ordering platform
   is a fixed product from R12,500 setup plus a monthly plan. You get an exact quote after a free
   discovery call."
2. "Do you build websites?" → "No. LP Web Studio builds business systems — ordering portals,
   client platforms, and automations. If you need a marketing website, we're not the right fit."
3. "Do you work with businesses outside Cape Town?" → "Yes. Discovery calls are online and
   projects are delivered remotely, anywhere in South Africa."
